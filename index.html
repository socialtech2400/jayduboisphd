ARC: practice where life pushes back.

ARC stands for Aware, Release, Choose & Commit. Jay and Winnie use it when a reply does not go the way they hoped.

What could that sound like?

With your partner

“I'm stretched thin in the evenings. Can we work out dinner and bedtime together? I'll take dinner tonight.”

With your ex

“I want us to decide on activities before either of us signs him up. Can we look at the cost and the days first?”

Each reply puts a specific need on the table. The practice continues in how you hear the answer and keep any agreement you make.

Ask Jay about the sessions ↗
What happens in the four sessions?

  1. Find where you get stuck

    Bring a recent disagreement. Identify what needed resolving, how you responded, and what remained unfinished. Choose one thing to work on.

  2. Prepare for the real reply

    Practice stating your need or boundary, then responding to disagreement. Use a brief breathing or movement practice to pause before answering.

  3. Review and follow through

    Look at what you said, what you heard, and what you did afterward. Adjust your approach, or rehearse if the conversation hasn't happened yet.

  4. Keep practicing

    Apply what you've learned to another situation. Leave with a personal ARC practice and a written plan for your next conversation and commitments.

Coaching focuses on your responses and actions. It is not couples therapy, mediation, or legal advice, and cannot guarantee another person's cooperation or the outcome of a relationship.

Inside the Book ↗

The Human Capacity Project asks what helps us flourish.

What does a teacher understand about growth? A physician? An artist? Each sees something different through the work they do.

Jay is interviewing people across fields to explore what human flourishing means from their perspective, which capacities support it, and how those capacities develop.

The inquiry is beginning with those conversations. It opens the question beyond any single practice: what else can we learn to become more capable in our relationships, work, and communities?
