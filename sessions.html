The conversation ended. Your response is still taking up the room.

You may be rehearsing what you should have said to your partner. You may be preparing for the next disagreement with your ex before it has even begun.

The replay uses attention you need elsewhere. The sessions begin with one real situation and the part of it that remains unfinished.

You cannot script the other person’s reply. You can practice how you will hear it, answer it, and follow through.

Four sessions. One situation brought into focus.

Each meeting works with what actually happened, what you need to say or decide, and what the other person may say back.

  1. 1

    Find where you get stuck

    Bring a recent disagreement. Identify what needed resolving, how you responded, and what remained unfinished. Choose one thing to work on.

  2. 2

    Prepare for the real reply

    Practice stating your need or boundary, then responding to disagreement. Use a brief breathing or movement practice to pause before answering.

  3. 3

    Review and follow through

    Look at what you said, what you heard, and what you did afterward. Adjust your approach, or rehearse if the conversation has not happened yet.

  4. 4

    Keep practicing

    Apply what you have learned to another situation. Leave with a personal ARC practice and a written plan for your next conversation and commitments.

ARC is practiced against a real reply.

Aware, Release, Choose & Commit gives you a way to stay in the conversation when agreement does not arrive on cue.

  1. Notice the reaction

    Catch the defense, interruption, or urge to go quiet.

  2. Make room

    Pause before answering so the reaction does not make the decision.

  3. Choose what comes next

    State your need, hear the reply, and keep any agreement you make.

With your partner

“I’m stretched thin in the evenings. Can we work out dinner and bedtime together? I’ll take dinner tonight.”

With your ex

“I want us to decide on activities before either of us signs him up. Can we look at the cost and the days first?”

A focused place to work on your part.

  • You are trying to change how you handle a recurring disagreement in your marriage.

  • You want to prepare for a conversation, review one that happened, or follow through on a commitment.

What the sessions are not

Coaching focuses on your responses and actions. It is not couples therapy, mediation, legal advice, or mental-health treatment.

It cannot guarantee another person’s cooperation or the outcome of a relationship.
