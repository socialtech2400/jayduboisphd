Why compare what different fields know about flourishing?

A project note on the question behind The Human Capacity Project.

The Human Capacity Project asks which capacities support flourishing and how those capacities are cultivated.

Every field learns something about human beings through repeated contact with real lives.

A physician sees people make decisions under pressure. A teacher watches curiosity grow, disappear, and return. An artist learns what sustained attention and disciplined imagination require. Parents and community leaders discover what trust, responsibility, and care look like when there is no clean theory to follow.

Much of that knowledge remains inside a profession, a community, or a person’s accumulated experience. It does not always travel. It may never be compared with what someone in another field has learned.

No single profession, discipline, or tradition has a complete view of human flourishing.

The comparison is the inquiry.

The project begins with conversations. Each person is asked how their path shaped what they understand, which capacities matter from where they stand, and how those capacities develop.

The aim is not to collect a list of impressive opinions. It is to preserve practical knowledge, place different perspectives beside one another, and notice where they reinforce, challenge, or complicate each other.

Stories show how a belief was earned.

Abstract answers matter, but experience gives them weight. A person may name patience, discernment, courage, curiosity, or responsibility. The next question is what convinced them that capacity matters.

A turning point can reveal the conditions around the capacity: a relationship, a practice, a place, a teacher, an institution, or a difficult period that demanded something new.

The questions stay open.

The interviews return to a small set of questions:

  • Which human capacities most reliably support flourishing?
  • How are those capacities cultivated through practice, relationship, environment, and experience?
  • What does this person’s field understand that another field may overlook?
  • Who else belongs in the conversation?
